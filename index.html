Acute Care Triage

When to Seek Immediate Emergency Care

Do not wait for an outpatient appointment if a patient presents with any of the following warning signs. Proceed immediately to an emergency department:

Cardiopulmonary Red Flags

  • Crushing retrosternal chest pain radiating to arm/jaw
  • Sudden dyspnea or SpO₂ < 92% on room air
  • Unexplained syncope or severe palpitations

Neurological Red Flags (FAST)

  • Sudden unilateral facial droop or limb weakness
  • Slurred speech (dysarthria) or aphasia
  • "Thunderclap" headache or neck rigidity

Infectious / Sepsis Warning Signs

  • High-grade fever with petechial rash or bleeding
  • Persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain, lethargy
  • Hypotension, cold clammy extremities, oliguria

Metabolic Emergencies

  • Fruity breath odor, Kussmaul breathing (DKA)
  • Diaphoresis, confusion, tremors (Severe Hypoglycemia)
  • Acute anaphylaxis with stridor or angioedema
